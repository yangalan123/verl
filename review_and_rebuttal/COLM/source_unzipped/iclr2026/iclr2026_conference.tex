\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) is a powerful paradigm for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs), yet its success hinges on effective exploration. An ideal exploration strategy must navigate two fundamental challenges: it must preserve sample quality while also ensuring training stability. While standard fixed-temperature sampling is simple, it struggles to balance these competing demands, as high temperatures degrade sample quality and low temperatures limit discovery. In this work, we propose a simpler and more effective strategy, \algname (\alg), grounded in the insight that exploration is most impactful on early tokens which define a sequence's semantic direction. \alg implements an intuitive \textit{explore at the beginning, exploit at the end} strategy by annealing the sampling temperature from high to low during generation. 
%
This dynamic schedule encourages meaningful, high-level diversity at the start, then gradually lowers the temperature to preserve sample quality and keep the sampling distribution close to the target policy, which is essential for stable training. 
%
We demonstrate that \alg is a lightweight, plug-and-play method that significantly improves sample efficiency, consistently outperforming fixed-temperature sampling across various RLVR algorithms and model sizes. 
%
Our work suggests that aligning exploration with the natural dynamics of sequential generation offers a robust path to improving LLM reasoning.
%
% Recent approaches to guide exploration have become increasingly complex and computationally expensive. 
% %
% Some methods rely on static, quantile-based entropy thresholds to make binary decisions about which tokens to update, while others employ expensive calculations, such as the covariance between log-probabilities and advantage functions, to closely regulate exploration at the token level.
% %
% % \ych{Sida: "micromanage" perhaps a bit vague and confusing}. 
% We argue that such complex, local heuristics might be unnecessary, as they fail to leverage the most fundamental dynamic of the generative process: 
% % as demonstrated by the ``Branching Factor'' (BF), 
% an LLM's output distribution naturally sharpens as generation progresses.
% %
% In this work, we propose a much simpler and effective strategy: \textbf{\algname (\alg)}.
% %
% Instead of granularly controlling specific tokens, 
% % we synchronize the exploration strategy with this global generation dynamic,
% we align the exploration strategy with the overall generation process.
% % \ych{"global dynamic" is not clear for readers.}. 
% %
% Specifically, our method employs a simple temperature schedule that starts high at the beginning of a rollout and gradually cools down, providing continuous control over exploration. 
% %
% This naturally front-loading exploration in the initial high-entropy steps prevents detrimental late-stage branching without requiring any complex token-level calculations. 
% %
% We show that this simple yet effective approach generates higher-quality trajectories for RL training on the fly, while adding minimal overhead and outperforming more complex methods.
% %
% More broadly, our findings suggest that aligning with the intrinsic dynamics of the model is a more direct and efficient path to improving LLM reasoning.
